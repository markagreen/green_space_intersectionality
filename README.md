# Exploring intersectional inequities in the impact of green space on mental health: An observational analysis of electronic health records for Cheshire and Merseyside (UK), 2024-2025.  

Repository for an upcoming paper.

**Abstract**

*Introduction:* Access to green space is an important barrier to mental health and can reduce inequalities. However, research investigating the equigenic effects of green space often focus on individual components which ignores that our social identities cannot be separated out. We utilise an intersectional approach to investigate equitable variations in the relationship between green space and mental health. 

*Methodology:* Electronic health records for all adults in Cheshire and Merseyside, UK (n = 1 996 247) were accessed. Our outcome measure was presence of a common mental health diagnoses (CMD). We linked at the household level distance to nearest green space. We used a MAIHDA framework through modelling intersectional strata defined by age group, sex, ethnicity and neighbourhood deprivation as a random effect in a multi-level model. We then extended this model by including distance to nearest green space as a random slope, allowing the association between green space and CMD to vary across intersectional strata. 

*Results:* Whilst 22% of individuals had a CMD, there was wide variation across intersectional strata. Ethnicity accounted for the largest variation in CMD. CMDs were also higher in females and more deprived areas. 19% of the variation in CMD was explained by the multiplicative effects of intersectional strata beyond their individual additive effects. When initially included as a fixed effect, people who lived further from green spaces were more likely to have a CMD. However, when we allowed this relationship to vary across intersectional strata this effect disappeared and was explained by differences across intersectional strata.

*Conclusion:* This study contributes to the literature through describing intersectional inequities of CMD and how access to green spaces shapes these relationships. Strategies to improve access to green space need to consider the different impacts across intersectional strata or consider tailoring interventions to benefit multiple social groups.
